01 · Idea

Do LLM agents propose the research a community goes on to pursue?

Freeze the literature at the end of 2025 and let LLM agents read it and write research proposals. In 2026 the same community publishes. Taste is how much the two overlap, in what they cite and in what they propose.

  1. Freeze the literature at 2025-12-31.
  2. Propose: LLM agents write research proposals from that snapshot.
  3. Compare with what the community published in 2026.

Abstract

Research taste, the judgment of what to read, which problems to pursue, and which methods to try, increasingly shapes the work of AI scientists, yet it remains far less understood than their ability to execute research. We introduce IdeaTaste, a benchmark that scores an LLM by how closely the research proposals it writes from a literature snapshot frozen at 2025 match the work the same communities published in 2026. Our experiments span six contemporary language models and four research areas from established to emerging fields, covering more than 90,000 proposals in total.

LLM agents fall well short of human communities in both the literature they cite and the ideas they state, reaching about 26% of the human level on citation matching and only 18% on idea matching. Their populations can cite as diversely as a community and take up its tasks and methods, yet rarely arrive at the concrete approaches and research plans it pursues. Neither richer field context nor larger populations substantially close this gap. In blinded expert review, AI proposals trail the 2026 papers least in presentation and most in expected success, echoing their gap in concrete approaches, and those whose ideas no human paper shares fare worst, suggesting weaker directions rather than overlooked opportunities. These results indicate that the main bottleneck in AI research taste lies less in finding the directions a community works on than in turning them into the concrete approaches it goes on to pursue.

The benchmark

Writing research means two choices: which prior work to build on, and what idea to state in relation to it. IdeaTaste compares LLM agents with a human community on both, using what the community actually did after the cutoff as the reference.

Frozen literature, future reference

For each area, papers published through 2025 form the corpus U that LLM agents search and cite. Papers published January–August 2026 form the human reference H. Both sets grow by following citations out from 20–22 hand-picked landmark papers per area. The reference is screened against a written area definition, and surveys are removed.

AreaCorpus UReference H

Two levels, four metrics

Each document is described by its citation set (cited papers in U) and by an idea summary in five slots, extracted from its abstract: contribution type, problem, motivation, proposed approach and research plan.

  • Match-Cite: harmonic mean of the nearest-neighbour Jaccard similarity of citation sets, proposals→papers and papers→proposals.
  • Diversity-Cite: sufficiency (citing enough) × spread (not citing the same papers).
  • Match-Idea: the weighted share of 2026-paper slots that some retrieved proposal matches, judged slot by slot. Rarely matched slots weigh more.
  • Diversity-Idea: one minus the weighted share of a proposal's slots that its neighbours already state.

Scoring the 2026 papers against one another gives the human level that every population is read against.

One LLM agent, four methods

All methods share one ReAct agent with a 20-step budget: plansearchread_papersdraftsummaryproposal. It can cite only papers it has retrieved. The methods differ only in what the agent knows about its area.

Free

Only told it is a researcher in the area. Reveals what it pursues without a prior.

Topic

Assigned one research topic, allocated in proportion to the corpus with recent years upweighted.

Landscape

Sees the area's most common task tags, method tags and task–method pairs with their shares, and can retrieve papers by tag.

Frontier

Topic, run in rounds. Later rounds can search and read earlier rounds' proposals and research traces.

Takeaways

01

Taste can be measured against the future

Instead of asking a judge whether an idea sounds novel, IdeaTaste asks whether the community went on to pursue it. The 2026 papers are the reference, and scoring them against one another sets the human level.

02

LLM agents stay far below that level

Across 96 populations they reach about 26% of the human level on citation matching and 18% on idea matching, even though their citations are as spread out as a community's.

03

The bottleneck is the approach, not the direction

Populations take up the problems and motivations a community works on, yet almost never arrive at the concrete approaches and research plans it pursues.

04

Scaling context or population does not fix it

Richer field context barely moves matching, and growing a population to the size of the human reference lifts it only gently. Unmatched proposals also fare worst in blinded expert review.

02 · Experiments

LLM agents reach a quarter of the human level

96 populations (6 models × 4 methods × 4 areas), 300 proposals each, scored against the 2026 papers.

  • Citations: LLMs reach ~26% of the human level.
  • Ideas: only ~18%; they find the right problems but rarely the right approaches.

01

The gap is in the ideas, not the reading

Averaged over all 96 populations, LLM agents reach about a quarter of the human level on citation matching and under a fifth on idea matching. Their citations are as spread out as a community's, often more so, but their ideas repeat one another.

02

Same problems, different approaches

Split by slot, populations match the problems and motivations of human papers many times more often than their approaches and research plans. Almost no human approach or plan finds a counterpart among the proposals. This holds for a model trained before 2026 (Llama 4 Scout) and for one released after (DeepSeek-V4-Flash).

03 · Analysis

Where the gap comes from, and whether it matters

  • More context? Barely helps matching.
  • More proposals? Helps slowly, still far below humans.
  • Are unmatched ideas hidden gems? No, experts rate them lowest.
03

More context does not help

Matching barely moves across methods, while diversity changes several-fold. Giving a direction (Topic, Frontier) spreads a population out; showing the full map of an area (Landscape) helps least of all.

04

More proposals help, slowly

Growing Free and Topic populations from 300 proposals to the size of the human reference (3.8–17× larger) lifts matching only gently. Even at equal size it stays well below the human level.

05

Unmatched proposals are weaker, not overlooked

Eleven reviewers scored 192 papers and proposals without knowing their source. Proposals trail the 2026 papers by only 4 points on presentation but 20 on expected success, so the gap lies less in how they are written than in whether they would work. It is widest for proposals whose ideas no human paper shares.

Same problem, different mechanism

For two 2026 papers, two proposals from one model share the problem. Only one of them also matches the approach.

Fetching uncached mixture-of-experts experts causes substantial I/O overhead.

2026 paperReMoE Fine-tune the router to favor recently used experts, reducing transfers without adding inference-time computation.
Qwen3.8-27B · FrontierApproach differs. Predict expert demand from prompt topics, then prefetch experts and coordinate caching without changing the model.
Qwen3.8-27B · TopicApproach matches. Train only the router to increase expert reuse over consecutive tokens while preserving language-model performance.

Pretrained world models must adapt to changing environments while retaining prior capabilities.

2026 paperSPREAD Freeze the pretrained model and update low-rank adapters online for rapid adaptation without replay.
DeepSeek-V4-Flash · TopicApproach differs. Keep weights fixed, store observed transitions, and combine retrieved predictions with the model's own.
DeepSeek-V4-Flash · FreeApproach matches. Adapt a frozen model through low-rank updates and compose adapters for combinations of dynamics changes.
